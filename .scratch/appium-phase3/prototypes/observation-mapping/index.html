Latency and its limits

The final Android run has 20 successful timed captures, five per state. Each source call returns XML before a separate PNG screenshot call. Source time excludes startup, screenshot, normalization, rendering, and file writes. Inclusive time covers both calls and the MCP round trip. The source and screenshot can therefore describe different moments during a transition.

The proxy trace localizes the cold source wait to UiAutomator2's GET /source. It does not separate the device's accessibility idle wait from tree traversal and XML serialization. Those internal costs remain unknown. The 10,000 ms idle setting is an upper bound, not a sleep added to every call. The source timing spread below is too wide for a platform speed claim. These are warm, sequential measurements on a 320 × 640 emulator with a small tree.

The earlier Android navigation attempt had one rejected lookup because the prototype used an identifier absent from the XML. Its ten captures are excluded. The baseline process lookup also failed once and was corrected before its captures. The iOS create failure is reported separately and is not a latency sample. Startup and full bridge settling were not benchmarked. The baseline measurements run later on the same private device, without Appium automation, so they support output comparison rather than a tuned speed comparison.

Completeness walkthrough

These buttons switch between two captures of the same Android state. Both parse as XML. The driver settings call set the shallow depth to 2 and then restored 70 with a readback. A small, well-formed tree is not proof of a complete tree.

Potential refusal signals for the owner to consider are invalid XML, an explicit driver error, a known node or byte cap, a leaf at the configured depth limit, or a deeper same-state capture that reveals extra nodes. Reaching a depth limit is suspicious; staying below it cannot prove completeness. Active-window selection, compression, and invisible-node exclusion can also omit evidence. Screenshots do not reveal offscreen or accessibility-hidden descendants.

In today's code, Android emits truncated: false. iOS detects only legacy compact limits, while production requests a full capture. renderAssertionState separately refuses text larger than 24,000 bytes with STATE_BUDGET. That protects the text budget without proving that the source was complete. This prototype renders with truncated: false only to exercise the renderer; completeness is explicitly unknown.

Coverage and evidence

Android covers two distinct pages and four state captures: no items, Apple selected, both items selected, and confirmation. Disabled controls, changing selection text, and actionable parents with text children are observed. The repeated parent and child labels come from the same displayed control. Duplicate independent controls with the same label, invisible controls, password fields, empty hints, a scrollable list, a large tree, and a continuously changing screen are not reachable in the frozen diagnostic app. Fields and scrollability are therefore unproven. Item-order permutations were not exhaustively explored.

The iOS historical samples cover the chooser before and after selecting Apple, including disabled controls and changing text. No fresh iOS feature coverage is claimed. Neither platform was rebuilt. Both exact private devices were shut down and checked again after use. Shutdown receipts and the frozen-file comparison are in the evidence directory.
